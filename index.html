The belief

Every kid who freezes at 7 × 8 already knows it — they just don't trust their own answer yet.

Confidence breaks the same way it's built: one answer at a time. A single wrong guess, called out in front of the class, is enough to convince a child they're "bad at math" — even when they already know most of their tables cold. Getting that trust back doesn't take more drilling. It takes practice that meets them exactly where the doubt is, and quietly lets them prove themselves right, again and again.

The method

Every attempt is timed and scored per fact, not per session — so practice can chase the one number that's still shaky.

Most flashcard apps drill the whole times table evenly. TimesTable tracks accuracy and response time separately for each of the 100 facts from 1×1 to 10×10, and quietly re-weights what comes up next: shakier facts appear more often and in easier formats, solid ones fade into occasional review, and the question format itself — straight recall, word problem, missing factor — shifts with how a child is actually doing.
